## 3. Design

### Population and setting

Define eligibility, sites, observation window, exclusions, and the unit of assignment and analysis.

### Comparison strategy

Describe randomization, matching, weighting, interrupted time series, pre/post comparison, descriptive benchmarking, or other planned design. State the estimand and its assumptions.

### Sampling and precision

Document the sampling approach, anticipated sample, clustering, detectable effect or precision considerations, and expected limitations.

## 5. Analysis plan

For each evaluation question, specify:

- analysis population
- model or descriptive method
- covariates and effect modifiers
- missing-data approach
- clustering and repeated-measure handling
- diagnostics and robustness checks
- subgroup analyses
- multiplicity approach when applicable
- software and version-control expectations

## 6. Qualitative component

Document sampling, protocol development, data collection, coding approach, triangulation, reflexivity, and privacy safeguards when qualitative work is included.

## 7. Data quality and provenance

Link each analytic field to its source, transformation, validation rule, owner, and approved version. Define reconciliation and issue-resolution procedures.

## 8. Ethics, privacy, and security

Record IRB or ethics determinations, consent needs, data minimization, access controls, disclosure review, retention, destruction, and incident escalation.

## 9. Interpretation and reporting

Describe how findings will be reviewed with subject-matter experts, how causal language will be governed, which audiences receive which products, and how limitations will be communicated.
