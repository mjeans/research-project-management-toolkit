# Nutrition-education evaluation: worked planning example

This is a **fictional, prospective planning example**, not a completed study, a funded project, or a claim about Matthew Jeans's prior work. Dates, roles, deliverables, and risks are illustrative. No participant data are included.

## Purpose and decision

A community partner is considering a 12-week nutrition-education pilot delivered through six community sites. The decision is whether the program and dietary-assessment process are feasible enough to justify a separately powered comparative evaluation. This pilot is not designed to establish effectiveness.

## Scope and evaluation questions

- **EQ01 — Reach and feasibility:** What fractions of invited, enrolled, and retained adults complete both dietary recalls at each assessment?
- **EQ02 — Measurement:** Can two 24-hour recalls per assessment support transparent fiber and sodium density summaries, with energy intake, completeness, and implausible-value review visible?
- **EQ03 — Delivery:** Which recruitment, scheduling, language-access, and staff-capacity barriers need resolution before scaling?

Within scope: protocol and estimands, accessible consent materials, assessment training, data dictionary, completeness audit, descriptive baseline/follow-up summaries, implementation interviews, and a decision brief. Outside scope: clinical dietary advice, individual risk classification, claims of causal program benefit, usual-intake estimation, or a new intervention trial. Any change to population, outcomes, or comparative design requires a documented change request.

## Decision rights and responsibilities

The fictional sponsor approves scope, resources, and the release decision. The evaluation lead owns protocol and interpretation. The nutrition-assessment lead owns instrument training and recall-quality rules. The data steward approves access and release controls. Community representatives review burden, language access, and reporting meaning. Applicable organizational ethics/privacy determinations must be obtained before recruitment; this template does not decide whether a real project needs a particular approval.

## Analysis and reporting guardrails

Report invitation, enrollment, assessment, and complete-recall denominators separately. Distinguish a within-person descriptive change from an intervention effect. Site clustering, attrition, dietary measurement error, energy adjustment, and recall-day variation must be addressed in the final approved protocol. Do not infer usual intake from two recalls alone. Suppression and complementary suppression rules require a data-steward decision before public release.

## Change and closeout

Material changes require the standard change-request template, impact review, sponsor decision, and updated requirements/work-plan links. Closeout requires accepted deliverables, unresolved risks with named owners, a reproducible artifact inventory, and approved retention/deletion instructions. No approval status is implied by this example.
